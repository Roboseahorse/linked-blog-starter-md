# Case studies

## Thanet Earth
Thanet Earth is the **largest complex of greenhouses in England**, located in **Thanet, Kent** in the **south east** of England. It is an example of **intensive commercial farming** in an MEDC,*more economically Developed country* , using advanced technology to grow food all year round.

### What is Thanet Earth?
- Thanet Earth covers approximately **55 hectares** — the equivalent of around **80 football pitches**
- It consists of **seven large greenhouses**, each dedicated to growing a specific crop
- Crops grown include **tomatoes**, **cucumbers** and **peppers**
- It uses **hydroponics** — growing plants without soil, using only nutrients and water in a solution
- The site supplies fresh produce directly to major UK supermarkets

### Key facts and figures:
- Produces **225 million tomatoes** each year — **12% of the UK's total production**
- Produces **13 million cucumbers** each year — **8% of the UK's total production**
- Employs over **500 people**, rising to **900 during seasonal peaks**, in an area with historically **low employment rates**
- Any **excess electricity** generated is fed back into the **UK's National Grid**
- Only **17% of fruit** and **55% of vegetables** consumed in the UK are grown domestically — Thanet Earth helps address this gap

### How does it work?
Thanet Earth uses a combination of technology and sustainable practices to grow crops efficiently:
- **Hydroponics** provides plants with precise nutrients and water, meaning no soil is needed and conditions can be fully controlled
- **Combined Heat and Power (CHP) generators** produce electricity to light and heat the greenhouses; excess power goes to the National Grid
- **Rainwater harvesting** collects and recycles water across the site, reducing water waste
- **CO₂ and hot air** from the power station are pumped into greenhouses to maximise plant growth
- **Bees** are used to pollinate plants naturally, and **natural predator bugs** are introduced to control pests, reducing the need for chemical pesticides
- **Computer-controlled environments** monitor and adjust temperature, light and humidity automatically

### Thanet Earth's influence:
- Thanet Earth won the **Net Zero Impact Award** at the FPJ Awards, recognising its environmental achievements
- It partnered with **Hadlow College** in Tonbridge, Kent to open the **UK's first Centre of Excellence in Greenhouse Growing**, training the next generation of horticultural workers
- It is seen as a model for **sustainable, large-scale food production** in the UK

## Advantages and Disadvantages of Thanet Earth

> [!success] Advantages
> - **500+ jobs** created in an area with historically low employment, rising to 900 at peak times
> - Improves the UK's **food security** — more vegetables grown domestically means less reliance on imports
> - Reduces **food miles** — produce is grown and sold within the UK rather than being transported internationally
> - **Excess electricity** is fed into the National Grid, benefiting the wider community
> - Uses **bees and natural predator bugs** instead of chemical pesticides, reducing environmental damage
> - **Hydroponics** means fertilisers are not needed, lowering chemical runoff into surrounding land
> - **Rainwater harvesting** reduces water waste and pressure on local water supplies
> - Crops can be grown **all year round**, something that would not be possible outdoors in the UK

> [!danger] Disadvantages
> - Greenhouses were built on existing **farmland and greenbelt land**, reducing the amount of natural countryside
> - **Natural habitats** have been destroyed during construction, harming local wildlife
> - The greenhouses are **lit at night**, causing **light pollution** for nearby residents and disrupting local wildlife
> - The **profits** go to large companies rather than back into the local community
> - Hydroponic systems are **expensive to set up and run**, which increases the cost of the food produced
> - The large scale of the site has a significant **visual impact** on the landscape of rural Kent

## Challenges and Solutions

### Food Security
The UK currently only grows **17% of its fruit** and **55% of its vegetables** domestically. This means the UK is heavily reliant on **imports**, which can be disrupted by global shortages, price increases, or political issues.

> [!check] Solution:
> Thanet Earth directly tackles this by producing over **10% of the UK's salad vegetables** domestically. By growing year-round in controlled environments, it reduces the need for imports and makes the UK less vulnerable to global supply chain problems.

### Skills Shortage
The UK horticulture industry faces a growing **skills shortage**, with not enough trained workers entering the sector to replace those retiring.

> [!check] Solution:
> Thanet Earth partnered with **Hadlow College** to create the **UK's first Centre of Excellence in Greenhouse Growing**. Students work directly with Thanet Earth in a commercial environment, gaining practical skills in modern greenhouse techniques and building careers in the sector.

### Environmental Impact
Large-scale greenhouse farming can damage the environment through **habitat destruction**, **light pollution** and **high energy use**.

> [!check] Solution:
> Thanet Earth addresses this through **Combined Heat and Power generators**, **rainwater harvesting**, **natural pest control** using bugs and bees, and feeding surplus electricity back into the **National Grid**. The company won the **Net Zero Impact Award** for its efforts to reduce its carbon footprint.
