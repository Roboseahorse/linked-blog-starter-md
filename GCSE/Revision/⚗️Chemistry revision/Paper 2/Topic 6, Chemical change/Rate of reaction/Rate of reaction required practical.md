## Objective:
To investigate the effect of changing concentration on the rate of reaction by measuring the formation of [[Calculating rate of reaction#^Precipitate|precipitate]]
![[Calculating rate of reaction#^Precipitate|precipitate]]
## Hypothesis:
A [[Calculating rate of reaction#^Precipitate|precipitate]] will be formed quicker if the concentration of the reaction is greater

## Materials
- 40 g/dm 3 sodium thiosulfate solution, Na<sub>2</sub>S<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub><sub>(aq)</sub>
- 2.0 mol/dm 3 dilute hydrochloric acid, HCl<sub>(aq)</sub>
- 10 cm 3 measuring cylinder
- 100 cm 3 measuring cylinder
- 100 cm 3 conical flask    
- White paper with cross marked on it
- Stopwatch

## Method
1. Measure 50 cm<sup>3</sup> of Na<sub>2</sub>S<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> solution into the conical flask
2. Place the white paper with cross marked on it underneath the conical flask
3. Measure 10 cm 3 of dilute HCl into a measuring cylinder
4. Add the hydrochloric acid into the flask and immediately start the stopwatch
5. Look down at the cross and stop the timing when the cross can no longer be seen
    - Solid sulphur is formed which precipitates in solution, turning cloudy:
    - Na 2 S 2 O 3 + 2HCl → 2NaCl + S + SO 2 + H 2 O
