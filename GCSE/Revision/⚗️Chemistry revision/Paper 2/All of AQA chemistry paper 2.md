## Rate of reaction
Any rate = change in quantity/ time (technically gives **MEAN** rate)
Can use mass/volume of reactant used/product made

To test for this react hydrochloric acid with sodium thiosulphate in a conical flask on a piece of paper with an x over it, as the reaction takes place the solution goes cloudy meaning the x is unable to be seen

Another way of measuring is to measure the volume of gas produced by attaching a gas syringe to the reaction vessel

Adding a catalyst in a reaction speeds up the reaction by lowering the activation energy. It does this by providing the reaction with an alternative pathway.

## Reversible reactions
- Both the forward and the reverse reaction will continuously take place
- Eventually the rate of both of these will be equal. **Equilibrium** is reached: the overall quantity of both sides will not change unless...
	- **Increasing pressure** will favour the reaction that produces fewer moles *The rate of this reaction will increase to produce more the substance with fewer moles until equilibrium is reached again*
	- **Concentration** follows the same principle *for example if it has reached **equilibrium** and we remove ammonia from the reaction vessel than more ammonia will be made* 
	- **Increasing temperature** favours the **endothermic** reaction *(as heat is needed for it!)* One direction is always **Endothermic**, while the other is **Exothermic**
