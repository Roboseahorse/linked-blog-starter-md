## Nuclear fission
- there is a lot of energy stored within the nucleus of an atom
- This energy can be released in a nuclear reaction such as fission or [[Nuclear fusion|fusion]]
![[1.1 - Energy Stores & Systems#^074e75|energy]]

- **Nuclear fission** is defined as - The splitting of a large, unstable nucleus into two smaller nuclei
- Isotopes of uranium and plutonium both undergo **fission** and are used as fuels in nuclear power stations
- During fission, when a neutron collides with an unstable nucleus, the nucleus splits into two smaller nuclei *(called daughter nuclei)* as well as two or three neutrons
- [[4.3 - Alpha, Beta & Gamma Radiation#^01985e|Gamma rays]] are also emitted

  

## Nuclear fission process

1. **absorption**: a slow-moving *(thermal)* neutron is absorbed by an unstable nucleus, such as uranium-235
2. Splitting: the resulting nucleus becomes highly unstable and splits into two smaller, roughly equal-sized nuclei *called daughter nuclei.*
3. Emission: the reaction releases 2 to 3 fast-moving neutrons and high-energy gamma radiation
4. Energy transfer: the products of the fission *(daughter nuclei and neutrons)* fly apart at high speeds, transferring the released energy into [[1.3 - Kinetic Energy|kinetic energy]]
  
![500](https://cdn.savemyexams.com/uploads/2020/02/nuclear-fission.png)

  

Large nuclei can decay by fission to produce smaller nuclei and neutrons with a lot of [[1.3 - Kinetic Energy|kinetic energy]]
  

- The products of fission move away very quickly  
    
- Energy transferred is from nuclear potential energy to [[1.3 - Kinetic Energy|kinetic energy]]

---
## Energy to electricity

In a nuclear power station, the massive amount of heat and kinetic energy produced by fission is harnessed to generate electricity:

1. thermal transfer: the [[1.3 - Kinetic Energy|kinetic energy]] of fission products and gamma rays is transferred to the reactors coolant (often water), raising its temperature.
2. Steam generation: the hot coolant is used to boil water, creating high-pressure steam
3. Turbines & generators: the steam turns massive turbines. These turbines turn a generator, which produces electricity.

## Spontaneous fission

- It is rare for nuclei to undergo fission without additional energy being put into the nucleus
- When nuclear fission occurs in this way it is called spontaneous fission

## Induced fission

- usually for fission to occur the unstable nucleus must first absorb a neutron
- *For example* uranium-235 is commonly used as a fuel in nuclear reactors
- It has a very long half-life of 700 million years
- This means that it would have a low activity and energy would be released very slowly
- This is unsuitable for producing energy in a nuclear power station
- Therefore during induced fission, a neutron is absorbed by the **uranium-235** nucleus to make **uranium-236**
- This is very unstable and splits by nuclear fission almost immediately
