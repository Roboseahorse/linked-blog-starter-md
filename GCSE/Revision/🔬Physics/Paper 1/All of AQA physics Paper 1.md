## Energy transfer
- In order for anything to happen energy must be transferred from one object to another or form one store to anther
- In a [[1.1 - Energy Stores & Systems#^0bf31f|closed system]] no energy is transferred to *(or from)* the surroundings

## Electric Circuits - [[2.3 - Charge, Current & Time#^49eeb9|charge]], [[2.2 - V = IR Equation & I-V Graphs#^e2a5d9|Current]] and [[2.2 - V = IR Equation & I-V Graphs#^Potentialdifference|PD]] 
- Electricity is the flow of [[2.3 - Charge, Current & Time#^49eeb9|charge]] or charged like electrons
- Energy is carried from a source of energy *like a battery* that has a [[1.1 - Energy Stores & Systems|store]] of chemical potential energy to a component *like a bulb* where the energy is released as another type of energy *in this case light*
- The movement of [[2.3 - Charge, Current & Time|charge]] is called [[Water analogy current and charge.png|Current]].
	- and we say this current goes from the positive terminal of the battery to the negative **current goes from positive to negative**
- As the circuit is one big loop the electrons that carry the charge are pushed back round to the battery by ones behind them, where they're re-energized, ready for another trip to the lamp
	- This constant flow of electrons transferring energy is what keeps the light on
- We cannot count individual electrons so we group them into coulombs of charge *(c)* 
- [[2.2 - V = IR Equation & I-V Graphs#^Potentialdifference|Potential difference or PD]] also known as [[2.2 - V = IR Equation & I-V Graphs#^Potentialdifference|Voltage]] tells us how much energy is transferred per coulomb of electrons/charge
	- We measure [[2.2 - V = IR Equation & I-V Graphs#^Potentialdifference|PD]] with a volt meter, voltmeters always get added last to a circuit and in parallel


## Resistance and Ohm's Law:
- components in a circuit have **resistance**, *that is*, they **resist the flow of charge or current going through them** 
- If we make a circuit with a resistor and change the [[2.2 - V = IR Equation & I-V Graphs#^Potentialdifference|PD]]  available to it, what we find is that an increasing [[2.2 - V = IR Equation & I-V Graphs#^Potentialdifference|PD]] results in a greater [[Water analogy current and charge.png|Current]] flowing.
- Resistance is measured in Ohm *(Ω)*  
![[2.2 - V = IR Equation & I-V Graphs#Diode]]

## Series and parallel circuit 

### Series Circuit
- Total [[2.2 - V = IR Equation & I-V Graphs#^Potentialdifference|PD]] Is shared between all component 
- [[Water analogy current and charge.png|Current]] Is the same for all components
- Total resistance = sum of resistance

### Parallel Circuit
- [[2.2 - V = IR Equation & I-V Graphs#^Potentialdifference|PD]] for each branch = [[2.2 - V = IR Equation & I-V Graphs#^Potentialdifference|PD]] pf cell/battery
- [[Water analogy current and charge.png|Current]] is split between the branches
- Adding more resistors in parallel Reduces the total resistance *because you are giving the [[Water analogy current and charge.png|Current]] more roots to move through the circuit, which means it can flow more easily*
