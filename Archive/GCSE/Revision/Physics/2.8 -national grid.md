
Giant network of transformers and wires that speeds across the country to distribute electricity.
Electricity comes from a range of different sources but most of it come from power stations.
Electricity demand peaks in the late afternoon and evening when everyone gets home from work/school

National grid has to transport huge amounts of power, To do this the national grid need to have a High voltage and High current (due to P=VI, power=voltage * Current)
To do this the national grid 
- uses step up transformers to increase the voltage to about ~400,000V (volts)
- The wires ten transmit the electricity all across the country through pylons 
- just before it reaches its destination (like a town) it passes through a step down transformer which reduces the voltage to about ~230V (volts) This is done for many reasons 
  1) A voltage too high is dangerous
  2) A voltage too high may blow our appliances


Fossil fuel and nuclear power stations work by generating thermal energy. This is used to turn water into steam which can then rise and turn turbine. The **kinetic** energy of the moving turbines can be converted to **electrical** energy, which is finally sent out across the national grid.
