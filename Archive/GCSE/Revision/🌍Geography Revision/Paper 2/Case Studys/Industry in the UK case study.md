# Case Studies

## Industry in the UK
The UK has the **6th largest economy** in the world. However, the sectors have **shifted in size** over time. The largest sectors of the economy are now:
- **Finance & Banking**
- **Construction**
- **Oil & Gas**
- **Healthcare**
- **Retail**
- **Education**

The industry has changed drastically over the last century. There are several case studies we can look at to understand this change.

## Torr Quarry
Torr Quarry is one of **eight limestone quarries** in **Somerset**. Quarrying began in the **1940s** and limestone remains in great demand across the UK — for **road construction**, in high-importance buildings, and to **treat waste and contamination**.

> [!success] Advantages
> - Quarrying provides **thousands of regular jobs** and a single quarry can contribute **£15 million** to a local economy each year
> - Transport of limestone is becoming more **environmentally friendly**, using trains and trams rather than lorries
> - Following quarrying, some sites are **restored to nature reserves**, increasing local wildlife diversity

> [!danger] Disadvantages
> - Quarrying involves **heavy machinery** and sometimes explosives, contributing to **noise and dust pollution**
> - **Agricultural land** is lost and **valuable habitats** can be destroyed during quarrying and road construction for transportation

## Hydrogen Industry
There is a growing industry of **hydrogen production** in the UK. Hydrogen is useful as it is a **[[Physics Paper 1 Masterclass#^renewable|renewable]] source of energy** and its production can be **carbon neutral**.
![[Physics Paper 1 Masterclass#^renewable|renewable]]
> [!success] Advantages
> - Hydrogen will contribute **£320 billion** to the UK's economy
> - Production of hydrogen will provide **120,000 jobs** — from machine operators to engineers
> - Being **carbon neutral** means no additional carbon dioxide or greenhouse gases will be released into the atmosphere, so hydrogen production will not contribute to **Global Warming**

> [!danger] Disadvantages
> - Hydrogen production involves large quantities of **energy** — it is not certain whether this energy will come from renewable or non-renewable sources
> - Hydrogen plants are constructed from **concrete, metals** and high-technology machinery that requires **precious materials**, all of which have their own environmental impacts
