# The concept of urbanization and global trends:

Urbanization refers to the increasing proportion of a population living in urban areas, such as towns and cities. more than half of the of the world's population lives in urban areas, this continues to rise. ^Urbanization

These areas are becoming more popular as places to live reflecting a significant shift in where people chose to settle globally. Urban areas are increasing attractive due to various social, economic, and infrastructural factors, driving consistent growth in urban populations worldwide

---
# Differences in urbanization rates across countries:

The rate and extent of urbanisation vary significantly between countries at different levels of economic development.

## Urbanisation in higher income countries (HICs)
- High urban population - Around 80% of the population in HIC's live in urban areas
- Slow growth rate - The rate of urbanisation is HIC is low, as most urbanisation has already occurred
- Counter-urbanisation trend - Many individuals in HICs move from urban to rural areas seeking less crowded quieter environments

## Urbanisation in lower income countries (LICs)
- Lower urban population - only about 30% of the population in LICs live in urban areas
- Rapid growth rate - LICs experiencing high rates of urbanisation due to ongoing migration and population shifts


## Urbanisation in newly emerging economies (NEEs)
- Moderate urban population - approximately 50% of the population in NEEs, where economic development is rapidly increasing, live in urban areas, through this can be higher in countries with significant urban growth 
- Moderate growth rate - The rate of urbanisation in NEEs typically stands at around 2% reflecting a balance between developing infrastructure in population movement

# Causes of urbanisation through rural-urban migration
**Rural-Urban migration**, ==The migration of people from countryside areas to cities== is a major driver of urbanisation. This migration is influenced by a combination of push and pull factors that encourage or discourage living in certain areas.

## Push factors driving people away from rural areas
- Impact of natural disasters - Events like floods or earthquakes can destroy homes and farmland making recovery difficult due to limited resources
- Mechanisation of agriculture - the use of advanced equipment reduces the need for manual labour, leading to fewer job opportunities in farming
- Desertification challenges - Land becoming unusable due to desertification
- Unstable farmer incomes - dependence of on successful harvests resulting in fluctuating earning, and financial insecurity 

## Pull factors attracting people to urban areas
- Greater job opportunities- cities offer more employment options often with higher wages
- Improved services - Access to better healthcare and education
- Family connection - The presence of relatives who have already relocated to urban areas can encourage others to follow.
- Perception of a better life - Many believe urban living offers an improved quality of life with more ammolites and opportunities

# The role of natural increase in urban growth 
Natural increase occurs when the birth rate surpasses the death rate, leading to population growth. This significantly contributes to the increase in [[13.1 - Global Urbanisation Patterns#^Urbanization|Urbanization]] 

## How natural increase boosts urban population
- **Youth migration** - young people often migrate to cities in search of employment, and then subsequently start families, adding to the urban demographic.
- **Healthcare** - Urban areas typically provide a better healthcare service which ultimately increases the life expectancy 
- **Population growth cycle** - The combination of higher birth rates among migrants and lower death rates due to improved healthcare prevents urban population decline

# The emergence and growth of megacities
Megacities are urban areas with populations exceeding 10 million people. They represent extreme examples of urbanisation, often emerging in regions experiencing rapid growth.

## Trends in megacity development
- current numbers - as of 2020, there was 34 megacities worldwide, with the majority located in LICs and NEEs
- Projected growth - By 2-36, the number of megacities is expected to rise to 48, reflecting ongoing urbanisation trends in developing regions.
- Significance - megacities highlight the concentration of population and resources in specific urban centres, often driven by economic opportunities and migration patterns
