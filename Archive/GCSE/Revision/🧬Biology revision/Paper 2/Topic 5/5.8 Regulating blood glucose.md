**Blood glucose concentration** - Way of describing the amount of sugar in our blood stream
We all need a decent amount of sugar in our blood so hat our cells have a constant supply of glucose making use for **respiration**, however if the **blood glucose concentration** gets to high it can start to damage out tissues, so we need to keep a fine balance where it is not too high or too low.

If you had a big meal, with lots of  carbs, it will be broken down in the **intestines**, it will be turned into **glucose** which it absorbed into the blood stream, and cause your blood glucose concentration to increase.

## Glucagon
- Hormone, that is released in the **pancreas**
- opposite of **insulin**
- Glucagon increases blood glucose levels
1. The pancreas detects low glucose concentration
2. The pancreas then secretes glucagon into the blood stream
3. as the glucagon travels around the body it binds to lots of different cells, but mainly liver cells
4. The liver cells take the **Glycogen** and break it down into glucose and then release the glucose molecules into the blood, bringing blood glucose levels back to normal

## Insulin & Glucagon
- Insulin and glucagon create a **negative feedback loop**
- if **glucose** gets to high **insulin** is released which makes glucose levels go down
- but if the **blood glucose levels drop** too low then **glucagon is released** which **increases the blood glucose levels**
