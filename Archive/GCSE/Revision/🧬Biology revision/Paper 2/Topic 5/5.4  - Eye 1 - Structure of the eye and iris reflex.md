## Structure of the eye
![[Pasted image 20260607103230.png]]
#### Cornea
- As **light** hits the eye the first structure it hits is the **cornea**, Cornea is completely transparent (and so has no blood vessels, this means that all the oxygen it needs has to diffuse into it from the outside air rather then being supplied from the blood ) to allow for all the light to get through
- The reason we need the **cornea** is that it causes all the light to refract/bend by a certain amount
#### Iris
- The iris is the coloured bit of your eye and controls how big or small the pupil is.
#### Pupil
- A gap in the middle of the iris that allows the **light** to pass through the **lens**
#### Lens
- Just the like the **Cornea** the job of the lens is to refract, but importantly the lens can change its shape to change how strongly it can refract the light, This helps it always focus the light onto the **retina**  at the back of the eye
#### Retina
- Made up of two different types of receptor cells

##### Rods
- More sensitive to light

#### Fovea
- Special spot in the retina which is filled with only cone cells, and it is this section that we try to focus light on so we can see most clearly
#### Optic nerve
- Takes all of the impulses generated by [[5.2 - The Nervous System, Synapses & Reflexes#^6f65fe|Receptor cells]] to the brain

## Iris reflex
- In very bright light conditions, the intensity of the light can actually cause damage to your retina, so to prevent this you have a reflex to control the size of your pupil
In bright light the pupil gets smaller to allow for less light to get in and to protect your retina. This is called **Constricted**
in low light the pupil gets larger to allow for more light to get in and allow for you too see more, in the dark. This is called **Dilated** 
- In order to make the pupil smaller in bright light conditions the **circular muscles** contract and the **radial muscles** slacken
