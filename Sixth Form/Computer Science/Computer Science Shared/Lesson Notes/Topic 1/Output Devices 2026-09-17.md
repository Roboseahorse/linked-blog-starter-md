## Learning Objectives

- Describe how different output devices can be used to solve different problems.
- Compare the features, advantages, and disadvantages of common output devices.
- Understand how output devices are used in monitoring and control systems.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> An **output device** takes data produced by a computer and converts it into a form that can be used by humans or other devices.

## What Are Output Devices?

Output devices:

- Convert computer data into a human-readable form.
- Can produce visual, audio, printed, or physical output.
- May also be used to operate another device, such as an actuator.

### Common Output Devices

| Device | Output Produced |
|----------|----------|
| Printer | Paper documents/images |
| Monitor | Visual display |
| Loudspeaker | Sound |
| Multimedia projector | Large visual display |
| Actuator | Physical movement |

---

## Inkjet Printers

### Key Features

- Most common type of printer.
- Available from low-cost home printers to professional photo printers.
- Some models use up to **10 different ink colours**.

### Advantages

- Good colour printing.
- Suitable for photographs.
- Low purchase cost.

### Disadvantages

- Ink cartridges are expensive.
- Some printers refuse to print black and white documents when a colour cartridge is empty.
- Water-soluble ink can smear when exposed to moisture.

---

## Laser Printers

### Key Features

- Use powdered ink called **toner**.
- Available in colour and monochrome versions.
- Widely used in businesses.

### Advantages

- Fast printing speed.
- Reliable.
- Excellent print quality.
- Cost-effective for large print volumes.

### Typical Uses

- Company stationery.
- Labels.
- Flyers.
- Brochures.

---

## Dot Matrix Printers

### Key Features

- Also called **impact printers**.
- Strike an inked ribbon to print dots onto paper.
- Similar operation to a typewriter.

### Advantages

- Can print multi-part forms.
- Works well in dirty or damp environments.

### Disadvantages

- Noisy.
- Lower print quality.
- Expensive to buy.

---

## 3D Printers

### How They Work

3D printers create solid three-dimensional objects by building them layer by layer.

### Materials Used

- Plastic filament
- Powdered resin
- Ceramic powder
- Metal powder
- Paper

### Applications

#### Medicine

- Prosthetic limbs
- Orthotics
- Surgical planning models
- Potential future organ printing

#### Manufacturing

- Replacement car parts
- Rapid prototyping
- Product design

#### Creative Industries

- Fashion
- Art
- Architecture models

> [!NOTE]
> A major advantage of 3D printing is the ability to create complex hollow structures that would be difficult using traditional manufacturing methods.

### Risks and Concerns

- Dangerous items could potentially be manufactured.
- Security concerns exist around printed weapons.
- Raises ethical and legal questions.

## RFID (Radio Frequency Identification)

### What Is RFID?

RFID systems are both **input and output devices**.

They use:

- A **receiver**.
- A **transponder (tag)**.

The receiver emits radio waves which power or communicate with the tag.

### Uses of RFID

- Contactless payments.
- Identification systems.
- Security checkpoints.
- Tracking goods in supply chains.
- Asset management.
- Potential replacement for barcodes.

### Passive vs Active RFID Tags

| Feature | Passive RFID | Active RFID |
|----------|----------|----------|
| Power Source | No battery | Battery powered |
| Range | Very short | Up to 300 m |
| Cost | Lower | Higher |
| Typical Use | Bank cards | Shipping and warehousing |

---

## Actuators

### What Is an Actuator?

An actuator is a motor-driven output device that produces physical movement.

### Examples

- Opening a valve.
- Opening a door.
- Starting a pump.
- Turning a fan.
- Moving an aircraft control surface.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Actuators are commonly used with sensors in **control systems**.

---

## Loudspeakers and Headphones

### Output Process

1. Digital data leaves the computer.
2. A **DAC (Digital to Analogue Converter)** converts the signal.
3. An amplifier boosts the signal.
4. The signal is played through speakers or headphones.

### Why a DAC Is Needed

- Computers process data digitally.
- Speakers work with analogue signals.
- A DAC converts digital signals into analogue signals.

---

## LCD Monitors

### Key Features

- LCD stands for **Liquid Crystal Display**.
- Pixels are made from red, green, and blue sub-pixels.
- LCD panels require backlighting.

### Backlighting Methods

- **CCFL** (Cold Cathode Fluorescent Lamp)
- **LED** (Light Emitting Diode)

The liquid crystals act like light valves and do not produce light themselves.

---

## LEDs vs CCFLs

| LED Advantage | Explanation |
|----------|----------|
| Faster brightness | Reaches full brightness quickly |
| Better image quality | Sharper images and vivid colours |
| Brighter display | Improved picture definition |
| Thinner screens | LEDs are very small |
| More reliable | Longer lifespan |
| Lower power use | Less heat and reduced running costs |

---

## OLED Screens

### What Is OLED?

OLED stands for **Organic Light Emitting Diode**.

### Advantages

- Thinner than LCD screens.
- Lighter than LCD screens.
- Flexible designs are possible.
- Brighter displays.
- Generate their own light.
- No backlight required.
- Lower power consumption.
- Produce less heat.
- Wider viewing angles.

### Common Uses

- Smartphones
- Tablets
- Smartwatches
- High-end televisions

---

## Multimedia Projectors

### Features

- Project text, images, videos, and presentations.
- Accept input from computers and media devices.
- Produce large display images.

### Typical Uses

- Presentations
- Classrooms
- Conferences
- Home cinema systems
- Gaming events

## Level Up

> [!QUESTION] ADC and DAC
> A temperature sensor records an analogue value of **28°C**.
>
> **a) Why is an ADC needed before the processor can use this data?**
>
> The processor can only work with digital (binary) data. An **ADC (Analogue to Digital Converter)** converts the analogue temperature reading into a digital signal that the processor can understand and process.
>
> **b) If the processor decides to switch on a fan, why might a DAC be required?**
>
> The processor produces a digital output. If the fan or control circuit requires an analogue signal, a **DAC (Digital to Analogue Converter)** converts the processor's digital signal back into an analogue signal.
