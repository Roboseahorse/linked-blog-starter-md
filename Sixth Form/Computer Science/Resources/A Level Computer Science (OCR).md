So this is the OCR spec, code **H446**. Three chunks make up the grade, and honestly they feel pretty different from each other once you're in them.

## The two exams

**Computer systems** is worth **40%**. This is the "how does a computer actually work" paper — processors, memory, storage, networks, how data moves around, plus a chunk on the legal and ethical side of tech. Two and a half hours, **no calculator**. It's a lot of recall but also a fair bit of "explain why."

**Algorithms and programming** is also **40%**, same length, same no-calculator rule. This one's more about thinking than memorising. You're solving problems, tracing through algorithms, writing code on paper (which is its own special kind of annoying), that sort of thing.

## The Programming Project

Then there's the last **20%**, the Programming project. You pick your own problem, build a working solution in whatever language makes sense for it, and write up the whole journey — why you chose the approach, how you tested it, what you'd change if you did it again. It gets marked in-house first and then OCR moderates a sample of the work.

## What it actually feels like

Less "learn to use software," more "learn how software gets made and why computers do what they do." There's real maths woven through it too — **Boolean logic**, **binary**, that kind of thing — so it's not purely a coding class, even though the coding is obviously the fun part.---
